# IconAid Benchmark Notes

These references inform visual language only. Third-party paths are not copied into IconAid.

## Professional Icon Systems

- Lucide: 24 px SVG icons with strict consistency rules, customizable color, size, and stroke width. License: ISC, with selected Feather-derived icons under MIT. Source: https://lucide.dev/ and https://github.com/lucide-icons/lucide/blob/main/LICENSE
- Tabler Icons: 24 by 24 grid, 2 px stroke, broad outline set, MIT. Source: https://tabler.io/icons and https://github.com/tabler/tabler-icons
- Phosphor Icons: flexible icon family with multiple weights and strong metadata, MIT. Source: https://github.com/phosphor-icons/core
- Heroicons: 24 px outline/solid plus smaller solid sizes, MIT. Source: https://github.com/tailwindlabs/heroicons
- Material Symbols: broad symbol system with fill, weight, grade, and optical-size axes, Apache 2.0. Source: https://developers.google.cn/fonts/docs/material_symbols?hl=en
- IBM Carbon: 16/20/24/32 icon usage, monochrome guidance, contrast requirements, strong grid discipline, Apache 2.0 package license. Source: https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/icons/usage/ and https://www.npmjs.com/package/%40carbon/icons
- Microsoft Fluent UI System Icons: familiar, friendly, modern icon language with multiple sizes and weights, MIT. Source: https://github.com/microsoft/fluentui-system-icons
- Font Awesome Free: broad practical concept coverage and strong naming, but SVG icons require CC BY 4.0 attribution. Source: https://github.com/FortAwesome/Font-Awesome
- Streamline: high-quality reference for coherent families and redrawn weights. License is proprietary; use as craft reference only. Source: https://home.streamlinehq.com/ and https://help.streamlinehq.com/en/articles/5900999-streamline-3-0-license

## Consulting Report Visual Language

- McKinsey strategy exhibits use simplified conceptual frameworks and restrained visual symbols paired with direct explanatory labels. Source: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/seeing-your-way-to-better-strategy
- BCG transformation material tends toward exhibit-first narrative graphics, simple symbolic grouping, and restrained visual hierarchy. Source: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2018/head-heart-hands-transformation
- Bain's Elements of Value uses a family of small, symbolic benefit icons organized in a framework. The lesson is family coherence and conceptual clarity, not copying its marks. Source: https://media.bain.com/elements-of-value/index.html
- Deloitte Tech Trends pages use polished editorial report graphics with larger visuals and limited symbolic icons. Source: https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/technology-management/tech-trends.html
- PwC CEO Survey uses data-heavy report presentation and chart visualization; icons should support interpretation, not compete with charts. Source: https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/issues/c-suite-insights/archive/ceo-survey-2025.html
- Accenture Technology Vision uses clear trend modules and simple symbolic entry points around AI, agents, space, and human interface themes. Source: https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/technology/technology-trends-2024

## Design Takeaways

- Use a small number of strong metaphors instead of literal object collages.
- Keep strokes, safe area, and apparent size consistent across categories.
- Prefer framework-compatible forms: targets, matrices, paths, ledgers, networks, shields, leaves, teams, and documents.
- Do not use generic base icon plus tiny badge variants as finished icons.
- Treat search metadata separately from artwork quality.
