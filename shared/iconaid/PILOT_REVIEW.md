# IconAid Pilot Review

The active 70-icon catalog is a schema and style-system checkpoint: the original 20-icon pilot plus five reviewed 10-icon expansion batches. It is not a completed catalog rebuild and must not be treated as a finished 425-icon catalog.

## Visual Findings

- Overall family coherence is improved: stroke rhythm, rounded joins, safe-area use, and apparent size are much closer across business, people, operations, security, and technology icons.
- Thumbnail clarity is acceptable for strategy, analytics, growth, organization team, customer, market, cloud, database, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, process, supply chain, factory, finance, risk, sustainability, document, and communication.
- Artificial intelligence was revised from an ambiguous brain/cloud mark to a chip-plus-model-graph mark. It is more recognizable at 24 px and aligns better with the technology family.
- Cost reduction was revised from a framed percent ledger to descending bars with a cost-down trend. It now mirrors the growth icon while remaining directionally distinct.
- Process was revised from generic connected boxes to a value-stream card with three clear stages. It now separates better from transformation.
- Transformation was revised to larger before/after state tiles with a single sweeping arrow. The 24 px read is now acceptable for the pilot.
- Finance is recognizable but generic. A later pass should separate corporate finance, investment, budgeting, margin, and cash-flow concepts rather than relying on a dollar mark.
- Factory is recognizable but close to the complexity limit. Additional operations icons should avoid adding more roofline details or small windows.
- The first framework batch adds roadmap, portfolio, matrix, decision tree, milestone, performance gauge, market outlook, ambition, value, and priority. The batch is coherent enough to keep, with roadmap, portfolio, matrix, decision tree, milestone, ambition, value, and priority reading clearly at 24 px.
- Performance gauge and market outlook are acceptable but weaker than the rest of the batch. They should be rechecked when dashboard and market-research families expand, because the gauge can become generic and the outlook icon can drift toward generic search.
- The finance batch adds capital, cash flow, budget, forecast, P&L, investment, tax, treasury security, pricing, and margin. Capital, investment, tax, pricing, margin, and P&L are the strongest marks in the batch.
- Cash flow, budget, treasury security, and forecast are acceptable but should be revisited when adjacent finance concepts expand. Cash flow can read as generic transfer, budget's small control mark is dense at 24 px, treasury security is close to a generic safe, and forecast overlaps visually with growth.
- The technology batch adds SaaS, microservices, data pipeline, AI agent, model, code branch, web application, sensor, digital twin, and integration. SaaS, microservices, code branch, web application, digital twin, and integration are the strongest marks in the batch.
- Data pipeline and model are acceptable but should be revisited as the data/AI family expands; both use node-link language that can become generic. AI agent is readable, but its face should stay restrained in future iterations so the technology family does not become cartoonish.
- The security batch adds firewall, key, certificate, compliance, access control, resilience, incident, privacy, audit, and security control. Key, certificate, compliance, access control, privacy, audit, and security control are the strongest marks in the batch.
- Firewall is acceptable and much stronger than a generic wall, but its shield overlay should be watched as security concepts grow so shields do not become the default answer. Resilience reads clearly as recovery/continuity but is close to the complexity limit at 24 px. Incident is intentionally adjacent to risk, but the signal strokes should stay limited to avoid noisy alert variants.
- The operations batch adds route, location, logistics, warehouse, quality, maintenance, inventory, procurement, service operations, and capacity. Route, location, logistics, warehouse, inventory, procurement, and service operations are the strongest marks in the batch.
- Quality is acceptable but should be watched as adjacent assurance/compliance concepts expand, because check-in-seal language can become generic. Maintenance was redrawn after the first contact sheet because it read as a pencil; the revised open-jaw tool is acceptable. Capacity is the weakest operations mark because it overlaps chart language, and should be revisited when utilization, throughput, and planning concepts expand.

## Proceed Decision

Do not rebuild all 425 icons in one procedural pass. The richer `elements` schema, editable primitive fallback, renderer migration, benchmark/licensing documentation, and contact-sheet workflow are ready for host-app testing and the next reviewed family batch. The next expansion should target communication and document icons, render another latest-batch contact sheet, and critique it before continuing.
